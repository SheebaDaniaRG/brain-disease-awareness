What is Brain Disease?

Brain disease (also called neurological disorder or brain disorder) refers to any condition that affects the structure, function, or health of the brain.

These diseases disrupt normal brain activity, causing problems with memory, movement, thinking, emotions, senses, or bodily functions.

The brain controls everything we do—breathing, heartbeat, thoughts, memories, movement, and emotions.

When brain disease occurs, it can damage brain cells, interrupt blood flow, cause abnormal cell growth, or create inflammation, leading to symptoms ranging from mild to life-threatening.

Key Characteristics

  • Can affect any part of the brain
  • May be acute or chronic
  • Can be reversible or progressive
  • Symptoms vary widely
  • Affects all age groups

Brain diseases are the leading cause of disability worldwide and second leading cause of death affecting over 3 billion people globally.

Risk Factors for Brain Disease

High blood pressure is the single most important modifiable risk factor for brain disease.

Air pollution (PM2.5) contributes to inflammation linked with Alzheimer's.

Smoking damages blood vessels and lowers oxygen delivery.

Diabetes and elevated blood sugar worsen neurological outcomes.

Obesity, inactivity and poor sleep affect brain health.

Stress, isolation and hearing loss increase risk.

Age, genetics and previous head trauma remain important factors.

Managing all major risk factors can dramatically reduce disease burden.
